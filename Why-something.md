I used to think the question of why there is something
rather than nothing was a bit of a meaningless
unusefull question that had no answer.
I have changed my opinion. I now suspect the answer
to that question is likely the foundation
to answering all other questions. 
If we understand why existence is inevitable,
we will likely then be able to derive all
that must inevitably exist and under what constraints.
It is not immediately appararent that space, time, or
matter should enevitably exist. so naturally, I feel the first
step to answering this question is by asking,
what can we conceive of that must exist.
The closest I can fathom, is the inevitable 
existence of mathematical truths. These truths exist in what we would generally consider abstractly.
Not physically. But abstract existence seems much less contingent than physical existence. The abstract  existence of the number 7 feels much less contrived
that atoms, particals, fields and literal space.
Sure, math relies on axioms. But given a set of axioms,
there are enevitable consequences that can be derived
and proven. That connection of axioms and proofs is what is inevitably true. even if the axioms themselvesare not true.
